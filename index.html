What the record shows — and what it doesn't

In every result

Legal name and DBA, USDOT and operating-authority status, MC numbers, power units and drivers, cargo carried, operation type, physical address and phone, and the MCS-150 filing date — exactly as FMCSA publishes them.

Insurance filing history

Filings that were cancelled or replaced, from FMCSA's open Insurance History data: insurer, policy number, form, amount, and dates. Useful for spotting gaps and churn in a carrier's coverage history.

Not in the open data

The carrier's current active filing is not part of that dataset, so we never guess at it. Each result links to FMCSA's Motus public snapshot, where the full Insurance Details — including the active filing — are public.

Questions

What is a US DOT number?

A US DOT number is the unique identifier the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration (FMCSA) assigns to a commercial motor carrier. Brokers, shippers, and insurers use it to pull up the carrier's safety, authority, and insurance records.

Where does this lookup data come from?

From FMCSA's public records: the SAFER Company Snapshot and FMCSA open data (the Company Census and Insurance History datasets). Every result shows the record's as-of date, and nothing is added or estimated — fields the source leaves blank are shown blank.

Why doesn't the lookup show the carrier's current active insurance filing?

FMCSA's open Insurance History dataset contains cancelled and replaced filings only, so that is all this page claims to show. The current active filing is in FMCSA's Motus public snapshot, under the authority's Insurance Details with "Include History" turned on — each result links straight to it.

Is this an official FMCSA website?

No. This site is run by Fifty Bridges Insurance Services, Inc., a licensed insurance agency. The data shown is FMCSA public data; the official source is safer.fmcsa.dot.gov.
