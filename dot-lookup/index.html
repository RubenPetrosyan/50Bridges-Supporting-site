DOT number lookup

Check a motor carrier's public record before you load them, lease on, or renew: USDOT and authority status, the FMCSA company snapshot, and the carrier's insurance filing history. The lookup reads FMCSA's own public systems — nothing is estimated or filled in.

Sources: FMCSA SAFER Company Snapshot and FMCSA open data (Company Census, Insurance History).

How to read the result

USDOT & authority status

Whether the carrier's registration and operating authority are active. An inactive authority means the carrier is not legally authorized to operate under it — a hard stop for brokers and shippers, and a red flag for everyone else.

The snapshot

Legal name, DBA, MC numbers, power units, drivers, cargo types, and operation classification, as the carrier reported them on its MCS-150. The MCS-150 date tells you how fresh that self-report is — carriers must update it every two years.

Insurance filing history

Cancelled and replaced filings: which insurer, which policy, what form and amount, and when it took effect and ended. Frequent changes or gaps can mean a carrier is struggling to keep coverage in force. The current filing is in FMCSA's Motus snapshot — the result links you there.

Checking your own record? If anything looks wrong — an old address, a wrong power-unit count, a filing you don't recognize — it is worth fixing before a broker or insurer sees it. Questions

How do I find a carrier's DOT number?

The DOT number appears on the carrier's insurance filings, rate confirmations, and the side of the truck itself. If you know the company name instead, search the FMCSA SAFER system by company name to find the number first.

What does "authority status" mean in the result?

It shows whether the carrier's federal operating authority (for example, Motor Carrier of Property) is active. A carrier whose authority is not active is not legally authorized to haul freight under that authority.

Why does the insurance history only show cancelled or replaced filings?

Because that is what FMCSA publishes as open data. The Insurance History dataset covers filings that were cancelled or replaced. The current active filing is public in FMCSA's Motus snapshot, under Insurance Details with "Include History" on — every result here links to it.

How current is the data?

The lookup fetches FMCSA records live and shows the record's as-of date. FMCSA data itself updates on the agency's schedule, so a very recent change — a filing made today, for example — may not appear yet.
